# JU.MP Web Design

## Cold Call Sales Training Manual

### Purpose of This Manual

This manual is designed to train JU.MP Web Design representatives to confidently introduce our services, explain the **$48/month website package**, handle common questions, and communicate what makes JU.MP different from traditional agencies, freelancers, and DIY website builders.

The goal of the cold call is **not to pressure someone into buying immediately.** The goal is to:

1. Get the business owner’s attention.
2. Explain what JU.MP does.
3. Clearly communicate the value of the $48/month package.
4. Answer basic questions.
5. Determine whether the business is interested.
6. Move interested prospects toward the next step.

---

# 1. The JU.MP Value Proposition

JU.MP Web Design works primarily with small businesses that need a professional online presence without the cost and complexity of hiring a traditional web agency or a full-time employee.

We provide an **ongoing website and branding solution**, rather than simply building a website once and walking away.

**Our core idea:**

**We sit in the middle between expensive agencies and doing everything yourself.**

A business owner generally has several options:

### Option 1 — Hire an Agency

A traditional agency can provide excellent work, but it can be expensive and may involve large upfront costs and ongoing fees.

### Option 2 — Hire a Freelancer

A freelancer may build the website for a one-time fee, but after the project is finished, the business owner may be left trying to find someone else whenever they need changes or additional work.

There can also be a branding problem when different freelancers create different materials at different times.

### Option 3 — Do It Yourself

The business owner can attempt to build and maintain everything themselves, but this takes time and requires them to learn website design, hosting, security, branding, and other technical skills.

### Option 4 — JU.MP

JU.MP provides an ongoing solution designed specifically for small businesses.

We help create and maintain the website while also creating a consistent visual identity that the business can continue using.

**The goal is simple: professional online branding without the cost and complexity of a traditional agency.**

# 2. The Standard Cold Call Script

Representatives should become comfortable with this script.

Do not sound like you are reading a script word-for-word. The script is a framework for the conversation.

### Opening

**“Hey, is this [Business Name]?”**

Once they confirm:

**“Hey [Business Name], my name is [Representative Name], and I work with JU.MP Web Design. I saw your business on Google Maps and noticed that you don’t seem to have a website, so I figured I’d give you a quick call to offer our services.”**

Then:

**“We partner with small businesses in order to run their websites and help create better online branding. We charge $48 a month, and that covers everything you would need to get your website up and running and maintained. Is that something you would be interested in?”**

Then **stop talking.**

Let the prospect respond.

# 3. The $48/Month Package

The representative needs to know exactly what the customer is receiving.

If the customer asks:

**“What exactly does the $48 cover?”**

The representative should be able to clearly explain the four major components.

## The $48/month package covers four major things:

### 1. Hosting, Domain Registration & Website Creation

The package includes the infrastructure needed to get the customer’s website online, including:

- Website hosting
- Domain registration
- Domain management
- Website creation

The customer doesn’t have to figure out how to purchase hosting, connect a domain, or manage the technical setup themselves.

### 2. SSL Certificates & Security Checks

The website includes:

- SSL certification
- Security checks
- Basic security maintenance

The purpose is to help keep the website secure and functioning properly.

---

### 3. Proactive Site Checks & Monitoring

JU.MP doesn’t simply build the website and disappear.

We proactively check and monitor the website.

This helps identify issues before the customer has to discover them themselves.

The value here is **ongoing management.**

The customer isn’t responsible for constantly checking whether their website is working properly.

---

### 4. Four Edits or Revisions Per Month

Customers receive:

**Up to four edits or revisions per month.**

This allows them to keep their website current.

Examples might include:

- Changing business information
- Updating service descriptions
- Changing contact information
- Updating text
- Making small website changes
- Updating images or other content

The important point is that the customer isn’t purchasing a website that becomes frozen after launch.

**They have an ongoing way to request changes.**

---

# 4. The Simple Way to Explain the $48

If the prospect doesn’t want a long explanation, use this:

**“The $48 covers four main things: your hosting and domain, SSL and security checks, proactive website monitoring, and four edits or revisions every month. So rather than paying someone to build a website once and then having to figure out maintenance yourself, we’re managing it for you on an ongoing basis.”**

This is one of the most important explanations in the entire sales process.

This is an important selling point.

**JU.MP does not use stock images as the foundation of its websites.**

Instead:

**We create custom illustrations and visual elements.**

This allows the website to have a more unique identity rather than looking like thousands of other websites using the same stock photography.

Representatives can explain it like this:

**“One thing that makes us different is that we don’t just throw stock images onto a template. We create custom illustrations and branding so the website actually feels like it belongs to your business.”**

# 8. Our Design Specialty

JU.MP specializes particularly in **landing pages and contact pages for service-based businesses.**

This is especially relevant to businesses such as:

- Contractors
- Detailers
- Landscapers
- Tree services
- Groomers
- Local service providers
- Other small service-based businesses

The objective is to make the website useful for turning visitors into potential customers.

A service business generally needs to answer a few basic questions quickly:

1. What does the business do?
2. Why should someone choose them?
3. What services do they provide?
4. Where do they operate?
5. How can the customer contact them?

A good landing page should make those answers easy to find.

# 9. Custom & Complex Websites

JU.MP can take on more customized and complex website projects.

However, our normal approach is to keep the core offering relatively **simple and lower-ticket.**

This allows us to focus on small businesses that don’t need a massive website or complicated system.

Representatives should **not promise complex features or special functionality** without checking with the appropriate person at JU.MP first.

If someone asks for something significantly outside the standard package, say:

**“We can definitely look at custom projects as well. I’d just need to get the details and have our team determine what’s possible and what the pricing would look like.”**

# 10. E-Commerce

**JU.MP does not currently offer e-commerce websites as part of its standard services.**

E-commerce websites can be significantly more complicated because they involve things such as:

- Products
- Shopping carts
- Payments
- Inventory
- Orders
- Customer accounts
- More complicated client management

Because of this, JU.MP has chosen to focus primarily on simpler service-business websites and branding.

If someone asks:

**“Can you build me an online store?”**

Do not promise that we can.

Instead:

**“We currently specialize more in service-business websites and landing pages rather than e-commerce. We can still take down your information and see if it’s something we can accommodate as a custom project, but e-commerce isn’t our standard offering.”**

# 12. Explaining the Problem We Solve

A representative should understand the customer’s problem before trying to sell the solution.

Many small businesses fall into one of three situations:

**“I don’t have a website.”**

They may be relying entirely on Google Maps, Facebook, Instagram, word of mouth, or other platforms.

**“I have a website, but it’s outdated.”**

The website exists, but it doesn’t represent the quality of the business.

**“I had someone build my website, but now I don’t know who manages it.”**

The freelancer finished the project and disappeared, or the customer doesn’t have an ongoing relationship with the person who built it.

JU.MP addresses that third problem particularly well.

# 13. The Key Difference: Ongoing Relationship

One of the strongest points to communicate is:

**JU.MP isn’t just a one-time website builder.**

We aim to become the business’s ongoing website and branding partner.

Instead of:

Build website → maintain website → monitor website → make ongoing edits → provide additional branding/design when needed

This gives the business owner someone they can continue coming back to.

# 16. Handling “I Already Have a Website”

Do not immediately argue with the customer.

Ask a question.

**“Gotcha. Are you happy with the website you have now?”**

If they say no:

**“What would you say you don’t like about it?”**

This gives the representative an opportunity to discover the customer’s actual problem.

Potential problems include:

- Website is outdated
- Website is slow
- Website doesn’t look professional
- No one updates it
- Branding doesn’t match
- They can’t easily make changes
- They don’t know who manages it

Then explain how JU.MP could potentially help.

---

# 17. Handling “I’m Not Interested”

Do not become aggressive.

Try one simple follow-up:

**“Totally understand. Just so I know, is it mainly because you already have someone handling your website, or is a website just not something you’re looking for right now?”**

The goal isn’t to pressure them.

The goal is to understand the objection.

If they’re clearly not interested, thank them and move on.

---

# 18. Handling “I Don’t Need a Website”

You can respond:

**“I understand. A lot of the businesses we talk to already get customers through referrals or Google Maps. The idea isn’t necessarily to replace what you’re already doing. It’s to give potential customers another place to see your services, learn about your business, and contact you while keeping your branding professional and consistent.”**

Then stop and let them respond.

---

# 19. Handling “I Don’t Have Time”

Keep it short:

**“Totally understand. I don’t want to take up your time. Would you be open to a quick follow-up at a better time?”**

The goal is to respect the business owner’s time.

---

# 20. Handling “How Much Does It Cost?”

The answer is simple.

**“Our standard package is $48 a month.”**

Then explain what it includes:

**“That includes hosting and domain registration, SSL and security checks, proactive site monitoring, and four edits or revisions each month.”**

Do not bury the price in a long explanation.

- Professional

### Don’t sound:

# 22. Don’t Over-Explain

A common mistake for new sales representatives is talking too much.

The opening should be short.

Explain the offer.

Then ask:

**“Is that something you would be interested in?”**

**Let them answer.**

If they say yes, continue the conversation.

If they have questions, answer the question.

Don’t dump every detail about JU.MP onto someone who hasn’t shown interest yet.

# 23. The Sales Conversation Structure

Think of the call as five stages.

### Stage 1 — Introduction

“Hey, is this [Business Name]?”

### Stage 2 — Reason for Calling

“My name is [Name], and I work with JU.MP Web Design. I saw your business on Google Maps and noticed you don’t seem to have a website…”

### Stage 3 — Offer

“We partner with small businesses to run their websites and help create better online branding. We charge $48 a month…”

### Stage 4 — Qualification

Ask questions and listen.

Examples:

“Do you currently have a website?”

“Are you happy with the website you have?”

“Do you currently have someone managing it?”

### Stage 5 — Next Step

If they’re interested, move the conversation toward the appropriate next step rather than continuing to talk endlessly.

# 25. What Not to Promise

Representatives must not promise things they aren’t authorized to promise.

Do not guarantee:

- A specific number of customers
- A specific amount of increased revenue
- Google rankings
- Guaranteed SEO results
- Complex functionality without approval
- E-commerce functionality
- Unlimited revisions
- Custom work for free
- A specific delivery date unless authorized

**Remember:**

**Sell the service. Don’t invent guarantees.**
